Speech synthesis

Speech synthesis is the artificial production of human speech.[1] A computer system used for this purpose is called a speech synthesizer, and can be implemented in software or hardware products for people who have lost their natural voice.

Early mechanical talking machines appeared in the eighteenth century, when inventors built bellows and reeds that could shape vowels well enough for a listener to guess the intended word from context alone.

Modern concatenative systems join short recorded fragments, while newer neural voices predict the waveform directly, which lets a phone read a whole article aloud in a voice that sounds almost human.

Evaluation usually asks listeners to rate naturalness and intelligibility, and the best systems now score close to recorded speech on short sentences drawn from everyday paragraphs.
